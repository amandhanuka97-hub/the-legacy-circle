The Library takes you through various opportunities in plain language as per your preference. You pick between Growth, Income and Asset-backed. The shelf does the rest.

Growth

Own it. A share is a piece of the business and its future.

Income

Lend to it. A bond is a loan: the business pays interest and returns your money.

Asset-backed

Finance it. A leased machine, an invoice, a building: the business pays for the use of your capital.

The physician building the first crore

Salary surplus, steady hands.

Three years of near-term goals sit in a short ladder of bonds and debt funds. The decade money compounds in equity funds on a monthly discipline. A small invoice-discounting allocation gives the first taste of financing a business, on tenures measured in weeks. Structure first, selection second.

Closest to your preference ₹50 lakh to ₹2 crore

The surgeon with no evenings

The practice absorbs the hours.

The school-fee years are covered by a ladder of debt funds. Growth runs through equity funds and a PMS mandate, so the selection is somebody's full-time job. One asset-leasing programme pays a quarterly income the household can feel. Time is an allocation decision too.

Closest to your preference Above ₹2 crore

The promoter after the exit

Concentration, unwound on purpose.

A bond ladder pays the household for five years without selling anything. Listed equity carries the compounding; a venture fund position is sized so a zero changes nothing. Leasing and trade financing add income that ignores the index. The second fortune is built slower than the first, deliberately.
